# Multiview Observation for Instruction-Guided 3D Part Removal

Scope: multiview 2D grounding, 2D-to-3D lifting, visibility and occlusion, and additional-view selection for Edit3D Step 6 M1 Phase 6.4. The notes distinguish published or officially documented evidence from Edit3D hypotheses. A 2D mask, video-tracking, part-segmentation, or scene-segmentation score is **not** evidence of source-face boundary correctness, complete removal, or body preservation.

## 1. How 2D masks are grounded, propagated, lifted, fused, and filtered

### Takeaway

The strongest reusable pattern is: preserve several text- or prompt-grounded 2D hypotheses; establish correspondence across calibrated views; lift only depth-visible pixels/samples; fuse evidence per face; then apply topology-aware filtering without allowing smoothing to invent semantic evidence. Text grounding and mask tracking solve different problems: grounding discovers candidates, while tracking propagates an already initialized identity.

### Cited Findings

#### Text grounding and cross-view propagation

- **Grounded SAM 2 is an engineering pipeline, not a distinct peer-reviewed 3D method.** Its official repository combines Grounding DINO/Florence-2/DINO-X detections with SAM 2/2.1 masks and video tracking; the authors explicitly state that it introduces no significant methodological change over Grounded SAM and provides simplified implementations. The basic video demo grounds only the first frame, while its continuous-ID mode periodically finds new objects but is marked “still under develop” and “not that stable.” Point, box, and mask initialization are supported; the repository says mask-derived sampled points give more stable tracking than raw boxes. It contains no calibrated-camera, mesh-face lifting, or 3D fusion stage. — [Grounded SAM 2 official repository](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2); [Grounded SAM technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14159)
- **SAM 2 propagates a prompted identity rather than discovering text concepts.** It accepts point/box/mask prompts and uses streaming memory for image/video segmentation. Official SAM 2.1 checkpoints range from 38.9M to 224.4M parameters and report 76.5–79.5 J&F on SA-V test, 71.8–74.6 on MOSE validation, and 77.3–80.6 on LVOS v2; these are video-object-segmentation metrics, not 3D face metrics. — [SAM 2 official repository and model table](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2); [SAM 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714)
- **SAM 3 directly addresses candidate discovery.** Promptable Concept Segmentation accepts a short noun phrase, positive/negative image exemplars, or both, and returns masks with unique identities for *all* matching instances; visual points/boxes/masks still address one object. The paper reports zero-shot mask AP 48.8 on LVIS versus 38.5 for the prior best and 30 ms for 100+ image detections on an H200, but also restricts text prompts to simple noun phrases and says behavior is undefined when later exemplars change the category definition. — [SAM 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719); [SAM 3 official repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3)
- **SAM 3 uses detector refresh to repair tracking failures.** It suppresses masklets with weak temporal detection support and periodically replaces tracker predictions with high-confidence detector masks to handle occlusion and distractors. Its paper also states that object-wise video processing has no shared object-level context, has cost linear in tracked-object count, and needs multiple H200 GPUs for real-time tracking at larger object counts. — [SAM 3 paper, video inference and limitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)
- **SATR grounds named semantic parts with detections, not masks.** It renders ten random mesh views, queries GLIP for each text label, maps box confidence to z-buffer-visible faces, area-weights projected faces, and aggregates across views. Gaussian geodesic reweighting suppresses box spill away from a “capital” face, and neighborhood visibility smoothing repairs central visible regions. SATR reports gains over its baseline of 1.3 mIoU points on coarse FAUST, 4 points on fine FAUST, and 5.2 points on ShapeNetPart. — [SATR ICCV 2023 project page](https://samir55.github.io/SATR/); [SATR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909); [official code](https://github.com/Samir55/SATR)
- **PartSLIP is a text-grounded point-cloud analogue.** It renders a point cloud from multiple views, applies GLIP part-name detections, and uses superpoints plus multiview voting/instance grouping to assign semantic and instance labels in 3D. The official repository provides Docker-based inference and a GLIP-L checkpoint path. PartSLIP++ adds SAM masks and maximum-likelihood multiview instance segmentation, but its official repository is separate and the original PartSLIP result should not be read as mesh-face evidence. — [PartSLIP CVPR 2023 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Liu_PartSLIP_Low-Shot_Part_Segmentation_for_3D_Point_Clouds_via_Pretrained_CVPR_2023_paper.html); [PartSLIP official code](https://github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/PartSLIP); [PartSLIP++ official code](https://github.com/zyc00/PartSLIP2)

#### Class-agnostic masks and 2D-to-mesh lifting

- **SAMesh preserves many candidate regions before deciding 3D communities.** For each regular icosahedral view it independently runs SAM 2 automatic mask generation on normal and shape-diameter renders, deliberately lowers SAM’s predicted-IoU threshold to 0.5 to preserve detailed/noisy masks, overlays masks from large to small, removes sub-1,024-pixel holes/islands and background, and records rendered triangle IDs. It does not use SAM 2 tracking because adjacent rendered poses differ more than the videos on which SAM 2 was trained. — [SAMesh paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [official code](https://github.com/gtangg12/samesh)
- **SAMesh associates masks through shared projected faces.** A graph node is one 2D region; two nodes are connected when both directional projected-face overlap ratios exceed a data-dependent threshold and at least 32 faces overlap. Leiden communities define 3D parts; each face receives its most frequently referred label. It then fills small unlabeled holes, expands adjacent labels, splits disconnected regions, and runs one graph-cut smoothing iteration. — [SAMesh paper, lifting method](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)
- **SAMesh evidence is mixed rather than uniformly superior.** On its curated human study, SAMesh ranked 1.176 versus ShapeDiam 1.824 (lower is better; 17 participants, five trials per mesh). On CoSeg it improved most metrics, but on the full Princeton benchmark it was worse on several metrics; after excluding four “pathological” categories (table, airplane, bird, mech), it became better. The authors say SAMesh favors surface delineation over component segmentation, can require parameter adjustment, and depends on SAM quality. Runtime is 30 seconds–1.5 minutes per mesh on a 30-core machine with one A10. — [SAMesh paper, Tables 1–5 and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)
- **GeoSAM2 converts sparse prompt control into multiview mask propagation.** It renders 12 normal and world-coordinate point maps at three elevations, lets a user click or box a target in one view, adapts SAM 2.1 Hiera-B+ with rank-4 LoRA and residual geometry fusion, keeps all view memories rather than SAM 2’s FIFO subset, and duplicates the first frame to bootstrap memory. The design explicitly targets sparse-view viewpoint jumps and occlusion that violate ordinary video assumptions. — [GeoSAM2 CVPR 2026 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Deng_GeoSAM2_Unleashing_the_Power_of_SAM2_for_3D_Part_Segmentation_CVPR_2026_paper.html); [official code](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/GeoSAM2)
- **GeoSAM2 performs explicit visibility testing when lifting.** It samples five points per mesh face, projects each point into all 12 masks, accepts a point only when its projected depth differs from rendered depth by less than 0.001, and votes visible labels; post-processing removes components smaller than roughly 1% of faces, optionally smooths connectivity labels, and k-nearest-neighbor fills unlabeled faces. — [GeoSAM2 paper, supplementary lifting procedure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)
- **GeoSAM2 reports strong class-agnostic part scores, with an optimistic evaluation protocol that matters.** It reports 84.06 mIoU on PartObjaverse-Tiny (PartField 79.18; SAMesh 56.86) and 74.42 on PartNetE (PartField 59.10; SAMesh 26.66). Vanilla SAM 2 gives 62.59/66.55; geometry adaptation and fusion raise these to 84.06/74.42. However, the evaluation tries four orthographic prompt views and retains the highest mIoU, and it automatically segments the opposite unlabeled region because one view cannot contain every part. The paper still lists heavy occlusion as a limitation. — [GeoSAM2 paper, Tables 1–3, protocol, and limitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)

#### Open-vocabulary 2D-to-3D feature lifting

- **OpenScene fuses dense 2D language-aligned features into 3D points.** It places 3D point, image-pixel, and text features in CLIP space, permitting arbitrary text similarity queries over scenes; its official code includes LSeg per-pixel feature extraction and multiview fusion. This is useful for semantic scoring, but it supplies dense scene semantics rather than instance-complete, removal-safe mesh parts. — [OpenScene CVPR 2023 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Peng_OpenScene_3D_Scene_Understanding_With_Open_Vocabularies_CVPR_2023_paper.html); [official code](https://github.com/pengsongyou/openscene)
- **3D-OVS shows that fewer views reduce semantic quality even when 3D distillation enforces consistency.** Its ablation reports 85.7 mIoU/95.7 accuracy using 50% of views and 79.1/94.6 using 10%; the authors describe 10% as only 2–3 views and roughly a 9% relative mIoU drop. Per-view multiscale CLIP features exceed 1 GB in the full model, and new text labels require retraining. — [3D-OVS NeurIPS 2023 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/a76b693f36916a5ed84d6e5b39a0dc03-Paper-Conference.pdf); [official code](https://github.com/Kunhao-Liu/3D-OVS)
- **Lift3D demonstrates general view-consistent lifting but is not a direct mesh-removal solution.** It trains a novel-view feature renderer on DINO/CLIP spaces and transfers unseen 2D operators, including open-vocabulary segmentation, without task- or scene-specific optimization. Its representation and benchmarks concern novel-view consistency, not exact source-face ownership or safe deletion boundaries. — [Lift3D CVPR 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/T_Lift3D_Zero-Shot_Lifting_of_Any_2D_Vision_Model_to_3D_CVPR_2024_paper.html)

### Inferences

- **Edit3D hypothesis—G1 candidate preservation:** use SAM 3/Grounded SAM detections and independent SAM proposals to create candidates, but retain low-threshold alternatives until 3D evidence is fused. This follows SAMesh’s successful “admit noise, filter after lifting” design and SAM 3’s exhaustive-instance interface; neither source proves removal safety. — [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [SAM 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)
- **Edit3D hypothesis—G2 complete/safe set selection:** compute separate per-face channels for semantic support, view count, visible projected area, cross-view agreement, and body-risk evidence. Do not collapse them into one vote before deciding whether a face is unsupported, contradicted, or simply unseen. SATR’s z-culling/visibility weighting and GeoSAM2’s depth test support this decomposition, but the proposed channels are project design, not a published Edit3D result. — [SATR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909); [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)
- **Edit3D hypothesis—G3 conditional refinement:** topology operations should be conditional on high semantic confidence and low visibility uncertainty. SAMesh hole filling and graph cuts can clean a known region, but its surface-versus-component failures show that connectivity cannot decide intent. — [SAMesh limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)

### Gaps

- No cited method evaluates instruction-correct complete 3D removal, source-face boundary precision/recall, retained-body damage, or Module 2 downstream behavior.
- No primary result found establishes that video tracking across large synthetic camera jumps is safer than independent per-view grounding plus calibrated 3D association. SAMesh explicitly avoids tracking for this reason, while GeoSAM2 requires geometry adaptation and full-view memory. — [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)
- GeoSAM2’s released repository contains inference code, configs, a checkpoint, and a small demo, but intentionally omits training/fine-tuning code; reproducing its reported training or adapting it to Edit3D therefore requires unreleased components. — [GeoSAM2 official repository](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/GeoSAM2)

## 2. More candidates versus more observations, and evidence for E4 OBSERVE

### Takeaway

“More candidates” changes the set of possible semantic objects or masks; “more observations” obtains new evidence about existing candidates from a different camera. E4 should add a view only when the unresolved error is plausibly caused by low visibility, cross-view disagreement, or under-represented target evidence—not when the available views consistently support the wrong semantic interpretation.

### Cited Findings

#### Operational distinction

| Operation | Adds candidates? | Adds observations? | Verified behavior |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Run SAM 3 with a noun phrase or new positive/negative exemplars | Yes | No, unless run on a new view | Returns all matching instances and can add/remove false-negative/false-positive objects through exemplars. — [SAM 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719) |
| Re-run Grounding DINO/SAM on the same render with prompt variants or lower thresholds | Yes | No | Produces more boxes/masks from the same pixels; Grounded SAM 2 exposes box/text thresholds. — [Grounded SAM 2 repository](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2) |
| Propagate one SAM 2 mask through a view sequence | Normally no | It transfers identity through existing frames | SAM 2 is prompted-object tracking; Grounded SAM 2’s simple demo initializes only one frame. — [SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714); [Grounded SAM 2](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2) |
| Render a calibrated novel view and run the same candidate queries | Potentially, if previously hidden instances become detectable | Yes | Adds visibility and projected-area evidence; SATR notes absent boxes can mean either invisibility or detector error. — [SATR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909) |
| SAMesh automatic masks on an additional view | Yes, at the 2D-region level | Yes | Adds new graph nodes and projected-face overlaps before community detection. — [SAMesh paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1) |
| Geometry-only smoothing/filling | No | No | Relabels existing faces; it does not observe hidden surfaces or resolve language. — [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036) |

#### Additional-view and occlusion evidence

- **Coverage alone is not enough for small semantic parts.** A 2026 Bayesian next-best-view preprint evaluates part discovery on 80 ShapeNet objects across 16 categories. Its task-specific discrete search required 2.93, 3.16, 3.43, 3.67, and 3.75 cameras to discover every class at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 target points, versus farthest-point coverage at 2.91, 3.30, 3.65, 3.85, and 4.11 and random at 3.29, 3.42, 3.65, 3.93, and 4.10. Qualitative examples identify a motorbike headlight and handbag handle that geometry-coverage strategies miss because those regions contribute little surface area. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095); [official code](https://github.com/jingsenzhu/BayesianNBV)
- **The same NBV paper exposes a key uncertainty trap.** Entropy-based acquisition can fail when the initial prediction is confidently wrong, because another view may change the label without lowering already-low entropy; cross-entropy acquisition that rewards a change in prediction is more robust. The authors recommend discrete candidate search as a practical default because gradient optimization was only slightly better or comparable in their tests. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095)
- **Explicit unseen-space uncertainty can distinguish occluded from observed regions.** A 3D Uncertainty Field initializes unknown voxels as uncertain and uses accumulated transmittance to leave behind-surface and outside-camera space uncertain. On Real-Occlusion, combined predictive-plus-unseen uncertainty obtains average AUSE 0.21, versus 0.31–0.67 for compared methods; training takes about 15 minutes per scene on one RTX 2080 Ti. Its NBV experiment begins with five views, scores each candidate by summed pixel uncertainty, and reports more informative selections than random, but the paper provides the PSNR comparison as a plot rather than a tabulated numeric delta. — [3D Uncertainty Field paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01815v1)
- **Learned informative-view selection outperforms indiscriminate density in scene segmentation.** A 2024 TIP method learns an information score from current 3D segmentation, clusters position-plus-normal space, and greedily renders views covering high-score regions. It improves several ScanNet/S3DIS base models by about 1.0–2.7 mIoU. Performance saturates beyond 60% informative-region coverage; four views per selected region saturate, yielding about 24 views for most scenes. The authors warn that redundant/contradictory views can reduce performance. — [Learning Virtual View Selection paper](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/papers/TIP-2024-ViewSelection.pdf)
- **Random camera coverage can leave holes.** SATR’s supplementary material states that its random sampling has no formal guarantee of covering every triangle and suggests a greedy method that ensures each triangle is selected or a fixed uniform set. — [SATR supplementary material](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~maks/papers/SATR_ICCV2023_sup_mat.pdf)
- **Tracking memory is not reliable occlusion uncertainty by itself.** DAM4SAM finds that SAM 2 updates memory even when the target is absent; short occlusion can fill memory with empty frames, reduce appearance diversity, and hurt reappearance. Merely blocking empty-mask updates improves DiDi tracking quality by 2.5%, while distractor-aware reliable updates improve quality by 4% over a five-frame update baseline. These are video-tracking results, not multiview mesh results, but they directly falsify the assumption that every propagated frame is useful evidence. — [DAM4SAM CVPR 2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Videnovic_A_Distractor-Aware_Memory_for_Visual_Object_Tracking_with_SAM2_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Edit3D hypothesis—E4 trigger:** OBSERVE is justified when target-adjacent faces have low visible-view count, low cumulative projected area, or high label disagreement *and* at least one candidate camera is predicted to expose them. It is not justified when multiple clear views agree on a semantically wrong candidate; that case requires G1 candidate expansion or G2 reselection. The distinction is inferred from task-specific NBV, SATR visibility, and SAM 3 candidate discovery, not directly validated for Edit3D. — [NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095); [SATR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909); [SAM 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)
- **Edit3D hypothesis—candidate-view score:** rank a finite camera pool by expected newly visible area of unresolved target candidates, expected reduction in cross-view disagreement, and under-represented semantic-part utility, minus expected body-spill exposure and redundancy. A finite pool is preferable initially because discrete search was competitive in the task-specific NBV evidence and is easier to audit. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095)
- **Edit3D hypothesis—three-way failure diagnosis:** (1) *unseen/under-observed* = low visibility support; (2) *ambiguous* = adequate visibility but disagreement among models/views; (3) *confidently wrong* = adequate visibility and agreement on a candidate inconsistent with instruction-level constraints. E4 primarily addresses (1), can help (2), and should not be expected to fix (3). The categories are an Edit3D control policy synthesized from the cited evidence, not a published taxonomy.
- **Edit3D hypothesis—stop rule:** stop observing when every selected target component reaches minimum evidence and marginal coverage/disagreement improvement falls below threshold; do not stop solely because semantic entropy is low. This follows measured saturation in virtual-view coverage and the confident-error failure of entropy-only NBV. — [Virtual View Selection](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/papers/TIP-2024-ViewSelection.pdf); [Bayesian task-specific NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095)

### Gaps

- No source found evaluates active additional-view selection for instruction-conditioned mesh-face removal or measures body preservation after the selected view.
- The task-specific Bayesian NBV work uses point-cloud scans and PointNet++ semantic outputs; transferring its utility to deterministic mesh renders removes geometric-reconstruction uncertainty and requires a different observation model. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095)
- The 3D Uncertainty Field and virtual-view-selection studies optimize reconstruction/scene semantics, not source-face ownership. Their quantitative gains establish that informative views beat random or redundant views, but not that their scores are calibrated for Edit3D. — [3D Uncertainty Field](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01815v1); [Virtual View Selection](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/papers/TIP-2024-ViewSelection.pdf)

## 3. Failure modes, implementations, and ranked applicability to Edit3D

### Takeaway

The best near-term evidence supports explicit z-buffer visibility, per-face multiview evidence, topology-aware spill control, and auditable finite-pool view selection. GeoSAM2 is the closest learned prompt-to-mesh system, while SAMesh provides the clearest candidate-preserving lifting recipe and SAM 3 the clearest exhaustive text-candidate interface. None should replace G2 safety checks or be treated as proof of removal correctness.

### Cited Findings

#### Failure modes relevant to removal

- **Thin/small parts:** task-agnostic coverage overlooks small but semantically important parts such as a headlight or handle; task-specific NBV improves discovery. SAMesh lowers the mask-quality threshold to preserve detail, but later removes small raster islands and small 3D communities, so thresholds can erase genuinely thin targets. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095); [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)
- **Repeated or similar instances:** SAM 3 is designed to exhaustively return all instances with unique IDs, and SAMesh argues that vocabulary-free parts can separate duplicates such as arms/hands. However, SAM 3’s tracker processes identities independently and reports no shared object-level context for crowded ambiguity; DAM4SAM identifies visually similar external and internal regions as major distractors. — [SAM 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719); [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [DAM4SAM](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Videnovic_A_Distractor-Aware_Memory_for_Visual_Object_Tracking_with_SAM2_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- **Texture-defined parts versus geometry-defined parts:** SAMesh reports that untextured renders do not yield sufficiently detailed SAM 2 parts and that normal plus shape-diameter renders rank best in its human ablation. GeoSAM2 improves textureless geometry through normal/point maps, while OpenScene provides language-aligned appearance semantics. Therefore geometry-only and appearance-only evidence are complementary; none of these papers proves the correct removal boundary when the instruction names a purely texture-defined region. — [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1); [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036); [OpenScene](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Peng_OpenScene_3D_Scene_Understanding_With_Open_Vocabularies_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- **Projection spill:** SATR explicitly observes unrelated faces repeatedly falling inside 2D boxes (“segmentation leaking”) and uses geodesic and visibility reweighting to reduce it. Mask methods reduce box spill but can still transfer raster boundary errors to faces; no cited paper reports source-face false-positive rates at attachment boundaries. — [SATR paper and Figure 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909)
- **Self-occlusion and incomplete visibility:** GeoSAM2 calls heavy occlusion a remaining limitation even with 12 views and geometry features. SATR notes that a missing detection conflates invisibility and detector error, and its random views do not guarantee triangle coverage. The Uncertainty Field shows standard predictive uncertainty can remain low in genuinely unobserved/occluded space. — [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036); [SATR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909); [3D Uncertainty Field](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01815v1)
- **Surface partitions versus removable components:** SAMesh’s four pathological Princeton categories show that coherent surface patches need not match component intent. Its authors explicitly prefer more parts and suggest user refinement for precision. This supports G1 over-preservation, but shifts the burden of safe completeness to G2. — [SAMesh results and conclusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)
- **View-sequence drift:** vanilla SAM 2’s FIFO memory assumes smooth temporal redundancy; GeoSAM2 reports that sparse view jumps make correspondence difficult and replaces FIFO with full-view memory. Independent tracking work shows unreliable occlusion/distractor frames poison memory. — [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036); [DAM4SAM](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Videnovic_A_Distractor-Aware_Memory_for_Visual_Object_Tracking_with_SAM2_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)

#### Ranked applicability

1. **Visibility-aware per-face evidence accumulation — highest immediate applicability to G1/G2/E4.** Reuse z-buffer/depth-consistency visibility, visible projected area, and per-face observation counts before any semantic fusion. This is low-invasion because it can sit upstream of the fixed mask interface and does not alter Module 2. Evidence: SATR and GeoSAM2 explicitly gate lifting by visibility. — [SATR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909); [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)
2. **Candidate-preserving multiview association — high applicability to G1.** SAMesh’s low-threshold 2D regions plus projected-face overlap graph gives a concrete way to preserve alternatives and associate them in 3D without prematurely selecting one text interpretation. Guard against its oversegmentation and hole-filling behavior. — [SAMesh](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13679v1)
3. **Exhaustive text-instance grounding with detector refresh — high applicability to G1 and repeated-instance diagnosis.** SAM 3 most directly separates “find every matching candidate” from “track this candidate,” while periodic high-confidence detection refresh addresses occlusion/distractor drift. Licensing and H200-scale reference compute lower deployment readiness. — [SAM 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)
4. **Finite-pool task-specific additional-view selection — high applicability to E4, but as a project hypothesis first.** Score camera candidates by newly visible unresolved target evidence rather than uniform surface coverage. The part-discovery evidence is directly relevant, but its point-cloud uncertainty model is not plug-compatible with deterministic mesh rendering. — [Bayesian task-specific NBV](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05095)
5. **GeoSAM2 prompt-to-mesh propagation — medium-high benchmark/prototype applicability.** It is the closest published architecture to one-view prompt → multiview masks → visible face labels and has strong reported mIoU. Its best-of-four prompt-view evaluation, opposite-side auto-segmentation, omitted training code, and heavy-occlusion limitation prevent treating it as a drop-in correctness guarantee. — [GeoSAM2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14036)
6. **SATR topology-aware spill filtering — medium applicability to G2/G3.** Geodesic and visibility weighting directly address projection spill, but boxes are coarse and topology can cross an attachment if the target and body are connected. Use as an auxiliary risk feature or conditional refinement, not sole selection. — [SATR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04909)
7. **OpenScene/3D-OVS dense language features — medium applicability to G2 semantic scoring.** They can score material, affordance, and long-tail semantics across views, but are weaker on instance completeness and require substantial feature memory or retraining. — [OpenScene](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Peng_OpenScene_3D_Scene_Understanding_With_Open_Vocabularies_CVPR_2023_paper.html); [3D-OVS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/a76b693f36916a5ed84d6e5b39a0dc03-Paper-Conference.pdf)
8. **NeRF unseen-space uncertainty / learned scene-view policy — conceptual E4 evidence, lower direct applicability.** Both show that occlusion-aware or task-aware views beat random/redundant views, but they add training/representation machinery disproportionate to a known mesh and do not evaluate removal. — [3D Uncertainty Field](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01815v1); [Virtual View Selection](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/papers/TIP-2024-ViewSelection.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Recommended Phase 6.4 experimental order (project hypothesis):** first log visibility and evidence diagnostics without changing masks; then test a finite E4 camera pool on cases classified as under-observed; only after that test candidate expansion (SAM 3/Grounded SAM prompt variants) and conditional topology refinement. This ordering isolates “more evidence” from “different hypothesis” and protects the frozen downstream interface.
- **Recommended E4 success criteria (project hypothesis):** measure incremental target-face recall, body-face false-positive rate, unresolved-face coverage, disagreement reduction, and final removal/body-preservation metrics separately. Do not use 2D AP, VOS J&F, or class-agnostic part mIoU as a proxy for source-face success.
- **Recommended safety rule (project hypothesis):** never fill unseen faces solely because they are connected to a selected component. Require either direct visibility evidence, consistent reverse-side evidence, or an explicit amodal-completion decision that remains distinguishable in logs.

### Gaps

- No reliable primary evidence was found for a method that jointly handles free-form instruction semantics, exhaustive repeated-part discovery, calibrated mesh lifting, active view acquisition, exact attachment-boundary recovery, and removal safety.
- Primary papers rarely report thin-structure recall, per-face calibration, or projection-spill false positives. Their aggregate mIoU/AP/J&F can hide the failure modes most costly for part removal.
- License status is not uniform: SAM 2, GeoSAM2, Grounded SAM 2, SATR, and OpenScene have explicit permissive repository licenses; SAM 3 uses a custom license; SAMesh shows no repository license in the official listing; PartSLIP’s own and transitive model terms require a dedicated audit before product use.
